1. READ THE COMPLETE LITERAL.
   Judge the whole control name, not a matched substring or prefix. A
   partial match to a keyword is not evidence by itself.

2. NUMBERING IS NOT CORRECTIVE EVIDENCE.
   A numbered suffix or index on an otherwise-ordinary bone/flex name
   (Bone01, Spine2_002, etc.) does not by itself suggest Correctives. Many
   numbered names are plain repeated/segmented anatomy or rig chains. A
   stale semantic_referent note guessing "(likely a deformation-
   corrective bone)" on a numbered bone does not override this rule --
   the guess itself is not evidence; look for actual repair/fix/correct
   language (rule 5).

3. TWIST/ROLL IS NOT CORRECTIVE EVIDENCE.
   "Twist" and "roll" bones are standard rig/deformation-helper anatomy
   (forearm twist, shoulder roll, etc.), not evidence of a corrective
   purpose by themselves.

4. "HELPER" IS NOT CORRECTIVE EVIDENCE BY ITSELF.
   A bare "helper"/"Helper" token in a name does not by itself indicate
   Correctives, nor does it indicate Useless/IGNORE (per the live
   philosophy's own text: "Helper/support naming alone is never sufficient
   reason for Useless or IGNORE"). Helper naming is neutral; look for an
   actual functional signal instead. This includes internal-looking or
   "_reference"-style naming and view-model/helper-adjacent controls (see
   rule 22's control-desirability note) -- none of these alone justify
   Useless/IGNORE or withholding a taxonomy destination.

5. EXPLICIT REPAIR/FIX/CORRECTIVE PURPOSE SUPPORTS CORRECTIVES --
   "COLLISION" ALONE DOES NOT.
   A literal that itself names a repair/fix/pose-correction PURPOSE (e.g.
   containing "fix", "correct", "repair", "JCM", or "squash/stretch
   correction" language) supports Correctives -- the corrective signal
   must come from the literal's own stated purpose, not from numbering,
   helper naming, or twist/roll alone (rules 2-4). The bare word
   "collision" is NOT by itself sufficient: collision geometry, collision
   physics, and collision-detection object naming are common non-
   corrective technical terms (hitboxes, physics proxies, trigger
   volumes). Only treat "collision" as corrective evidence when the
   complete literal also states a repair/compensation PURPOSE for that
   collision (e.g. "collision-fix", "collision-compensate"), not merely
   that collision is involved.

6. ORDINARY STRUCTURAL ANATOMY GOES TO ITS ANATOMICAL DESTINATION.
   A control that simply names and postures a real anatomical
   region/bone goes to that region's established group (Face subgroup,
   Arms, Legs, Body, Hair, Wings, Tail, etc.) under ordinary posing intent.
   This includes a bare numbered segment, a side marker alone, or an
   explicit "root"/"parent"/"origin" bone designation on an otherwise
   plain anatomical noun -- none of these describe the visible form
   actively changing (contrast rule 7).

7. BODY MORPHS IS PURPOSE-BASED, NEVER A KEYWORD TRIGGER.
   Body Morphs contains controls whose COMPLETE LITERAL indicates
   intended body-shape, size, soft-tissue form, appearance, or
   morphological/state deformation of the character's OWN body.
   A direction/action word (up, down, forward, backward, movement, poke,
   pull, push, tighten, grip, open, close, inflate, deflate, bulge, wide,
   skinny, thick/thickness, shrink, muscular, tone, hard/erect,
   smack/impact-deformation, augmentation/"job", detail) is CONTEXTUAL
   EVIDENCE ONLY -- it is NEVER a sufficient keyword trigger by itself.
   Do not generalize "anatomical noun + up/down/open/etc. = Body Morphs":
   that would wrongly catch ordinary articulation controls that share the
   same vocabulary for genuinely different reasons (EyeUp, JawOpen,
   ArmForward, FingerGrip, TongueForward, ShoulderDown are all routine
   POSING controls for regions that have real skeletal/articulation
   function, not morphs).
   The test: does the region named have a genuine skeletal/articulation
   function that this word could instead be describing as ordinary
   posing? If yes (an eye, a jaw, an arm, a finger, a tongue, a shoulder),
   treat the word as posing evidence, not morph evidence, absent a
   further explicit shape/size/state word. If no (soft tissue with no
   joint of its own -- nipple, breast, buttock, belly, genitals), a
   direction/state word describing its form/position can only represent
   an intended appearance/state change, and Body Morphs applies.
   PLAIN STRUCTURAL ANATOMY (rule 6) -- a bare body-part name, a numbered
   segment, a side marker alone, an explicit root/parent/origin
   designation -- is never Body Morphs regardless of the region.
   Object ownership still wins over this entire rule: object-specific
   shape/fit/deformation routes to the object's own group (rule 8), even
   when the visible effect sounds like a body-shape change (e.g. a pants
   feature that happens to expose or reshape an anatomical opening stays
   with the pants unless the anatomical control is clearly the primary
   subject and the object is only a triggering context).
   LUBE/WETNESS/FLUID EFFECTS ARE NOT BODY MORPHS MERELY BECAUSE THEY ARE
   ADULT-CONTENT OR ANATOMY-ADJACENT. A bare lube/wetness/fluid-effect
   control is an effect/material/fluid referent, not structural anatomy
   and not anatomical shape/deformation morphology -- it routes to
   groupFile/Other on that basis. Do NOT generalize this into "every
   control containing 'wet' is Other" -- if the complete literal clearly
   describes an anatomical geometry/state change alongside a wetness
   word, classify by that full anatomical meaning instead.

8. OBJECT-SPECIFIC SHAPE/FIT/DEFORMATION STAYS WITH THE OBJECT'S
   DESTINATION.
   Applies generally, not only to shape descriptors: a control that
   exists to fit, repair, hide, or accommodate a specific animator-facing
   object (garment, prop, accessory) belongs with that object's own
   group, even when the underlying mechanism resembles a body-shape or
   corrective control. When a literal names both a body region AND an
   object, ask which is the PRIMARY SUBJECT and which is only a
   triggering/contextual qualifier (e.g. "AnusOpenSuitExposedTorn": the
   anus is the primary subject, the torn suit is only the triggering
   context, so this stays a body/anatomy control; "PantsAnalOpen": the
   pants are the primary named object being given an opening feature, so
   this stays with Clothing).

9. GENERIC DCC/RIG PREFIXES DO NOT DETERMINE DESTINATION.
   Prefixes such as bip_, Bone_, DEF-, ORG-, MCH- and similar generic
   rigging/DCC-tool naming conventions carry no destination signal by
   themselves. Judge the remainder of the literal. See rule 24 for the
   closely related model/character-prefix rule and rule 23 for the
   abbreviation-grounding standard that governs short codes appearing
   within such prefixes.

10. EXPLICIT SIDE MARKERS DETERMINE LEFT/RIGHT SUBPATHS -- ONLY WHERE
    THAT SUBPATH IS INDEPENDENTLY VERIFIED FOR THE CLASS OF CONTENT
    INVOLVED.
    Do not infer that every Left/Right arm or leg control must enter a
    LeftArm/RightArm-style child group merely because such a child group
    exists in the Master's structure. Use a side-specific subpath only
    where the live taxonomy's actual, verified content assigns that
    CLASS of control there (confirmed by direct Master inspection:
    LeftFingers/RightFingers, LeftCarpals/RightCarpals, LeftToes/
    RightToes all hold substantial, genuinely side-specific content). An
    ordinary anatomical control (a plain arm or leg bone, even explicitly
    sided) may legitimately remain directly under the bare Arms/Legs
    destination despite its explicit L/R spelling -- side information is
    preserved in the literal itself and does not require a matching
    structural subpath to exist. When uncertain whether a Left/Right
    child group holds verified content for a given control class, default
    to the bare parent group.

11. NO SIDE MARKER -> USE THE SIDE-NEUTRAL DESTINATION IF ONE EXISTS.
    When no side marker is present and the group structure offers a valid
    side-neutral destination (a bare group with no Left/Right split, or a
    groupColor/shared-control tier), use it rather than guessing a side.

12. THE THREE ABSTENTION STATES ARE NOT INTERCHANGEABLE.
    Do not guess to force a CLEAR result, but pick the state that matches
    WHICH question is unanswered:
      - if NO token in the complete literal names any real object,
        material, or concrete function/action (a bare number, a pure
        positional/directional adjective with no object noun, a generic
        DCC/software auto-name artifact, or an ambiguous short
        alphanumeric code) -> semantic_status = OPAQUE, taxonomy_status =
        NOT_APPLICABLE. These two are biconditional (rule 21) -- OPAQUE
        must never be paired with NEEDS_CONTEXT or NEEDS_DOCTRINE, and
        NOT_APPLICABLE must never be paired with CLEAR or PARTIAL
        semantic_status.
      - if the semantic referent is understood (CLEAR or PARTIAL --
        something real is named) but its ownership or exact destination
        is not established by the complete literal -> taxonomy_status =
        NEEDS_CONTEXT (semantic_status stays CLEAR or PARTIAL as
        independently warranted -- see rule 22).
      - if the semantic referent and general destination category are
        both understood, but resolving it requires a standing policy
        decision this rulebook does not yet make (a genuine cross-family
        ambiguity, not a single literal's missing information) ->
        taxonomy_status = NEEDS_DOCTRINE, reported rather than guessed.
    A semantic_status=CLEAR row must never carry a semantic_referent or
    uncertainty_reason that contradicts the referent it asserts (e.g.
    confidently naming a specific object while simultaneously saying that
    object's domain cannot be established) -- that combination means the
    row should be PARTIAL, not CLEAR (rule 22). Before finalizing any
    batch, mechanically spot-check CLEAR rows with hedging language in
    semantic_referent or short_reason for exactly this contradiction.

13. SETTLED: BARE STRUCTURAL "HEAD"/"SKULL" DEFAULTS TO BODY.
    A bare head/skull reference with no specific facial feature named
    (no eye/brow/cheek/jaw/etc. compound) defaults to groupFile/Body,
    consistent with existing Master practice for bare structural/
    positional anatomy. If the complete literal names a specific facial
    feature (e.g. "head eyelid left"), that feature's own Facial Taxonomy
    destination governs instead -- the word "head" there is a location
    prefix, not the control's true referent.

14. SETTLED: "SPINE" IS RESOLVED BY OWNERSHIP, NOT BY DEFAULT AND NOT BY
    TREATING EVERY SPINE LITERAL AS AMBIGUOUS.
    "Spine" is confirmed multi-referent in this Master's own history (real
    anatomical spine vs. corset/dress design element vs. hair-physics-chain
    nickname), but that history does not make every spine literal a
    doctrine gap -- the complete literal usually establishes ownership:
      - an explicit anatomical/biped spine literal (Bip01_, DEF-, bip_,
        SK_, or similar generic skeleton/rig prefix + "spine," with no
        garment/hair-object noun present) -> groupFile/Body
      - an explicit object-owned spine literal (names a garment/design
        element, e.g. a corset "spine"/ribbone, or a hair-physics-chain
        nickname) -> that object's own semantic destination (Clothing,
        Hair, etc.)
      - a bare/referent-free "Spine"/"Spine01"-style literal where the
        complete literal gives no ownership evidence either way ->
        NEEDS_CONTEXT
    Do not invoke the historical collision to justify flagging a spine
    literal that DOES carry ownership evidence as a doctrine question.

15. SETTLED: SELF-DECLARED "UNUSED" (OR "DUPLICATE") GOES TO USELESS,
    REGARDLESS OF WHETHER THE REST OF THE LITERAL IS ANATOMICALLY
    READABLE.
    An explicit self-declared "unused" (or "duplicate") marker in a
    literal is positive Useless-evidence and takes priority over the
    control's otherwise-readable anatomical referent, per already-
    executed Phase 1 precedent. This applies regardless of the anatomical
    region or species named. This is NOT limited to cases with an
    identifiable body part: Phase 1 precedent already includes purely
    generic self-declared-unused controls with no anatomical content at
    all (e.g. "unused trash 01", "unused trash 02," already live in
    Useless). A self-declared unused/duplicate placeholder ->
    groupFile/Useless (taxonomy_status CLEAR) even when semantic_status
    is only PARTIAL (e.g. an unused generic bone carrying unrelated
    attribution/credit text, or a bone explicitly flagged "duplicate...
    unused"). The self-declaration alone is sufficient taxonomy evidence;
    it is NOT a NOT_APPLICABLE case (rule 21).

16. RECOGNIZED PROPS/WEAPONS/EQUIPMENT GO TO OTHER -- WORN EQUIPMENT
    (INCLUDING HOLSTERS) DEFAULTS TO CLOTHING.
    Other already holds, by strong and directly verified existing
    precedent, the vast majority of controls naming recognized real-world
    objects that don't fit a character-specific semantic group: weapons
    and weapon parts, ammunition, vehicles/furniture mechanisms, doors,
    books, pillows, and similar handheld/mechanical/furniture props. This
    is applying an existing, well-evidenced Master convention, not
    creating new taxonomy -- do not add a dedicated Props category.
    WORN-EQUIPMENT EXCEPTION: an item whose primary real-world function is
    to be worn ON the body follows Clothing's "worn accessories" rule
    instead of Other. This includes helmets and, per settled policy,
    HOLSTERS: a holster is treated as worn equipment and routes to
    groupFile/Clothing by default when the literal describes it as
    character equipment/body-positioned gear (e.g. Gear.Holster,
    l_side_holster_*, l_rear_holster_*, Holster_Base) -- Clothing, NOT
    Other, unless the COMPLETE literal positively establishes that the
    holster is a component of a specific weapon/prop ASSEMBLY rather than
    worn character equipment (e.g. explicitly named as a sub-part of a
    particular gun/sword prop hierarchy). Apply this settled default
    without re-litigating it per family. Carried/handheld/mechanical/
    furniture items without a worn-equipment reading remain Other.

17. AN IMPLEMENTATION-STYLE SUFFIX/PREFIX NEVER DETERMINES LEGITIMACY OR
    DESTINATION -- READ THE NAMED OBJECT.
    A suffix or prefix such as _mesh, _bone, _ctrl, _jnt, DEF-, bip_,
    Bone_ (including generator-style prefixes like BLD_GEN_, DEC_GEN_,
    FUR_GEN_, LOOT_GEN_) does NOT by itself determine whether a control is
    legitimate, and does NOT by itself determine its destination. The
    scanner having surfaced the item as an effective control is sufficient
    -- do not exclude or hold it merely because its name resembles a
    DCC/export implementation identifier. Read the complete literal for
    the named object instead:
      - a clearly named prop/environment/object component (tent, wagon,
        bed, pillow, etc.), regardless of a _mesh-style suffix -> classify
        by the named object, normally groupFile/Other where no more
        specific destination applies
      - a clearly named WORN object/accessory (e.g. a backpack), regardless
        of a _mesh-style suffix -> groupFile/Clothing, per rule 16's worn-
        item exception
      - a generic/opaque identifier with no named referent at all (e.g.
        "Mesh01") -> follow rule 12's abstention test
    See rule 24 for the closely related rule on model/character-prefix
    naming specifically, which extends this same "read past the prefix"
    principle to abbreviations that stand for a model, character, or
    variant rather than an implementation detail.

18. SPECIES-NEUTRAL BODY-PART WORDS NEED NO CREATURE-SPECIFIC DOCTRINE --
    BUT VERIFY PRECEDENT PER WORD, AND NEVER INFER WING OWNERSHIP FROM
    SPECIES ALONE.
    Words that already name a real, species-neutral anatomical
    region/bone/covering (brow, fur, horn, fin, tentacle, femur, an
    individual digit/finger/claw/toe segment) route to their existing
    established Master category the same way for a creature as for a
    human, using the SAME word-level precedent check as any other
    anatomy word -- no creature-specific taxonomy is needed or permitted.
    Confirmed by direct precedent (do not assume without checking):
      - an individual digit/finger/claw/toe-segment control on a creature
        routes to the existing Fingers/Toes destination, matching the
        human-equivalent structure
      - a WHOLE paw/limb-end assembly (not a specific digit) does NOT
        belong in Fingers merely because the bare word "paw" happens to
        be majority-Fingers historically in the live Master -- a whole
        paw is the limb-end assembly, not a digit; route it to the plain
        limb destination (e.g. groupFile/Arms) instead, consistent with
        rule 10's caution against assuming a Left/RightArm subpath
      - a named leg-end "toe" on a creature routes to the existing Toes
        destination the same way a human toe would
      - horn and tentacle route to Other, matching existing precedent
      - femur has no wing-vs-other competing reading in any vertebrate
        (it is always a hindlimb/leg bone), so femur routes to Legs
        without any of the following cautions applying
    DO NOT INFER WING OWNERSHIP MERELY BECAUSE THE ANIMAL IS A BIRD (OR
    ANY OTHER FLIGHTED CREATURE). A bone routes to Wings only when the
    COMPLETE LITERAL, or an established and documented skeletal-taxonomy
    reference for that specific model, sufficiently identifies it as part
    of the flight appendage -- not merely because the species in question
    has no other forelimb type. A clavicle, humerus, ulna, or similar
    shoulder-girdle/forelimb bone on a bird is NOT automatically Wings by
    elimination. Phalanges is explicitly NOT unqualified: birds have
    phalanges in BOTH the wing (digit bones) and the foot (toe bones), so
    a bare "phalanges" literal does not, by itself, establish wing
    ownership over foot ownership. If the literal does not itself say
    "wing"/"flight"/name a wing-specific segment, and no other
    established evidence resolves it, use NEEDS_CONTEXT rather than
    Wings for any of these ambiguous bone types.

19. LIMB COUNT AND SPECIES DO NOT CHANGE SEMANTIC DESTINATION.
    The taxonomy is semantic/function-based, not limited to a conventional
    two-arm/two-leg human skeleton. Where the literal clearly names the
    function, limb count is irrelevant to destination: a third/additional
    arm -> groupFile/Arms (with an explicit side marker determining the
    LeftArm/RightArm subpath only where rule 10's verified-subpath test is
    met); a front/back/hind leg -> groupFile/Legs; a left/right toe (any
    species) -> the corresponding Toes subpath; eye -> Face/Eyes; ear ->
    Face/Ears; tail -> Tail; wing -> Wings (subject to rule 18's caution
    against inferring Wings from species alone). Do not create creature-
    specific taxonomy, and do not treat "this literal implies more than
    two of this limb" as doctrine-worthy by itself.

20. A BODY-PART WORD WITH NO ESTABLISHED REFERENT REMAINS NEEDS_CONTEXT --
    THE GAP IS OWNERSHIP/CONTEXT, NOT SPECIES OR LIMB COUNT.
    A term such as bare "Pincer" remains NEEDS_CONTEXT when the complete
    literal does not establish whether it is anatomical, mechanical,
    weapon-related, or something else, and no existing Master precedent
    exists for the word in any direction. Flag NEEDS_CONTEXT for this
    reason specifically -- missing ownership/context in the literal --
    not because the referent might be nonhuman or because it names more
    than the standard limb count (rule 19 already settles those).

21. SCHEMA INVARIANT -- semantic_status=OPAQUE AND taxonomy_status=
    NOT_APPLICABLE ARE BICONDITIONAL.
    NOT_APPLICABLE means "there is no semantic content for a taxonomy
    question to even apply to" -- it is defined by, and only by, the
    semantic meaning itself being opaque. Therefore: semantic_status=
    OPAQUE MUST imply taxonomy_status=NOT_APPLICABLE, and taxonomy_status=
    NOT_APPLICABLE MUST imply semantic_status=OPAQUE. There is no
    legitimate exception where NOT_APPLICABLE coexists with CLEAR or
    PARTIAL semantic_status, and OPAQUE must never be paired with
    NEEDS_CONTEXT or NEEDS_DOCTRINE (see rule 12). If a literal has ANY
    partial semantic content (e.g. a self-declared "unused"/"duplicate"
    marker, even with no anatomical referent), a taxonomy question DOES
    apply to it and taxonomy_status must be CLEAR / NEEDS_CONTEXT /
    NEEDS_DOCTRINE -- never NOT_APPLICABLE. Before finalizing any batch,
    mechanically check this invariant across all rows
    (semantic_status == "OPAQUE") == (taxonomy_status == "NOT_APPLICABLE")
    and treat any violation as a miscoding to fix, not as evidence of a
    new exception category.

22. SEMANTIC_STATUS AND TAXONOMY_STATUS ARE INDEPENDENT AXES.
    semantic_status answers "how completely do we understand the
    literal's semantic referent/function?" taxonomy_status answers "is
    the semantic information we DO have sufficient to determine one
    Master destination?" PARTIAL semantic_status paired with CLEAR
    taxonomy_status is VALID and EXPECTED -- it is the normal outcome
    whenever the PRIMARY owner is clear enough to route the control but a
    secondary qualifier, action, or token genuinely remains undecoded. A
    taxonomy decision becoming CLEAR must NEVER mechanically or
    conceptually upgrade semantic_status to CLEAR. Before marking a row
    semantic_status=CLEAR, re-read its own semantic_referent and
    short_reason: if either still explicitly says a meaningful qualifier,
    action, owner, function, or token is unresolved (hedging language
    such as "unclear," "unspecified," "not explicit," "not fully clear,"
    "possibly," "likely" used to hedge on IDENTITY rather than mere
    spelling origin), semantic_status must be PARTIAL, not CLEAR --
    regardless of how confidently the taxonomy destination was resolved.
    Conversely, do not downgrade taxonomy_status merely because
    semantic_status is PARTIAL: rows whose semantic referent really is
    fully understood may remain CLEAR, and rows whose PRIMARY owner is
    clear despite an undecoded secondary detail keep their resolved
    taxonomy destination (see rule 12's abstention test for the case
    distinctions).
    A hedge on a COSMETIC spelling-origin guess (e.g. whether a leading
    "H" means "Half," whether "Ubber" is a misspelling of "Upper") is NOT
    a semantic-completeness gap when the control's actual FUNCTION is
    fully understood either way -- semantic_status may remain CLEAR in
    that case. Likewise, uncertainty about whether a control is a
    DESIRABLE animator control (an internal/helper/viewmodel/reference-
    looking control that may not be exposed to animators) is NOT the same
    as uncertainty about its semantic taxonomy or its semantic
    completeness -- neither downgrades semantic_status nor blocks a
    taxonomy destination when the owner is otherwise clear (see rule 4).

23. AN ABBREVIATION EXPANSION IS NOT SEMANTIC EVIDENCE UNLESS GROUNDED BY
    A DEMONSTRABLY COHERENT SIBLING SERIES.
    Do not upgrade a literal's semantic_status merely because an
    abbreviation COULD plausibly expand to a meaningful word. An
    abbreviation may support a semantic interpretation only when its
    expansion is grounded by at least one of:
      - other explicit content in the SAME literal (e.g. RForeArmEX is
        readable because "ForeArm" supplies the referent -- "EX" alone
        inherits nothing from this by itself)
      - a recognized rig/DCC naming convention with SPECIFIC, not merely
        plausible, support
      - sibling spellings in the SAME corpus establishing the expansion --
        meaning a DEMONSTRABLY COHERENT naming series or template, not
        merely another unrelated literal elsewhere that happens to
        suggest the same expansion. A true series shares a consistent
        structural template with no unexplained format divergence (e.g.
        Key.Black_1..Key.Black_N, Key.White_1..Key.White_N, Key.0-Key.9,
        Key.A/Key.B, Key.CapsLock, Key.Enter, Key.Colon, Key.Comma all
        share the identical period-separated "Key.<label>" template with
        no descriptive suffix -- a genuine, coherent keyboard-prop
        series). A superficially similar but structurally different
        literal is NOT a sibling merely because it shares a substring or
        theme: a SPACE-separated "Key 17" is not a sibling of the
        period-separated "Key.<label>" series merely because both contain
        "Key" -- if the space-separated form belongs to its OWN coherent
        series (e.g. "Key 1," "Key 1 - Shirt," "Key 3 - PoneyHair" reading
        as a keyframe/pose-preset series), that is a DIFFERENT family with
        a different likely meaning, not evidence for the first family's
        expansion. Likewise, "whL_2" and "wheelback_base"/"fake_wheel_fl"
        are NOT siblings merely because both could involve "wheel" -- they
        share no naming template at all, only a thematic guess.
      - strong, specific corpus evidence (a documented reference, not an
        inferred guess)
    A merely plausible expansion, with no such grounding, is not enough --
    leave the literal governed by rule 12's abstention test (typically
    OPAQUE/NOT_APPLICABLE).

24. A MODEL/CHARACTER/VARIANT PREFIX IS NOT ITSELF SEMANTIC OWNERSHIP --
    CLASSIFY WHATEVER INDEPENDENTLY-GROUNDED CONTENT REMAINS.
    Ignore a prefix that names or abbreviates a model, character, rig, or
    variant (e.g. TaurenMale_HD, Bip_STONE, fbx_sakura) as a semantic
    owner unless it is positively established otherwise, then classify
    whatever MEANINGFUL, independently-grounded content remains in the
    rest of the literal. This is NOT limited to anatomical content:
      - TaurenMale_HD_bone_78 -- if the remainder is only a generic
        numbered technical bone and "HD" is an ungrounded abbreviation
        (rule 23), this stays OPAQUE/NOT_APPLICABLE; a long, contiguous,
        generically-numbered bone series after the prefix is itself a
        strong signal that the prefix is a model/variant tag, not a
        body-region label, and "HD" must not be expanded to "head" merely
        because that expansion is possible
      - TaurenMale_HD_Cape01 -- "Cape" is meaningful, independently-
        grounded content despite the model/variant prefix, so this
        classifies as Clothing regardless of what "HD" means
      - fbx_sakura_face / fbx_murasaki_face -- "face" remains meaningful
        despite the DCC/model prefix, so this routes to the bare
        groupFile/Face destination (consistent with rule 13's treatment
        of bare structural anatomy with no further named feature)
    Do not require the remaining content to be anatomical specifically --
    require only that it be meaningful and independently grounded.

25. OWNERLESS DECORATIVE-ACCESSORY TERMS DO NOT DEFAULT TO CLOTHING.
    A bare decorative-accessory noun (strap, tassel, string, charm, band,
    bow, and similar) with no named owner does not default to Clothing
    merely because the item CAN be worn -- it can equally plausibly be
    jewelry, a weapon/prop accessory, or mechanical hardware. Use the
    explicit owner where present:
      - Wakazashi.Tassel_* -> weapon/prop ownership -> Other
      - an explicit garment name (Pants string, boot tassel, a "necklace"/
        "pendant" that is spelled out, a body-location anchor such as
        "belly button" for a piercing) -> Clothing
      - a bare term with no such owner and no strong standing Master
        precedent resolving it (Strap_B.02, TasselB, Band.Bend_Right_1, a
        bare "bow," a themed "charm" that names only its theme and not
        its mount) -> semantic_status PARTIAL, taxonomy_status
        NEEDS_CONTEXT, destination blank
    Do not over-correct obviously wearable terms with an unambiguous
    single reading (veil, headgear, necktie) into false uncertainty --
    this rule targets genuine multi-category ambiguity, not every
    accessory noun. Apply this test to EVERY Clothing-destination
    candidate with a bare decorative-accessory noun, not only to the
    first instance encountered in a batch -- consistency across an entire
    batch is part of the rule, not an afterthought.
