METHODOLOGY (read this before the numbers)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Per-family classification was performed by decomposing every exact literal in
the family into its constituent words (splitting on punctuation, camelCase
boundaries, and letter/digit boundaries, with a compound-word fallback for
concatenated lowercase forms such as "bottomlid" -> "bottom"+"lid"), then
reading the FULL set of resulting words against a curated multi-domain
dictionary (anatomy, clothing, hair, facial expression, body-morph
descriptors, creature/species terms, generic positional/functional words,
and known rig/DCC technical markers). This is a mechanical implementation of
"read the whole literal," not a lookup of prefix, suffix, token frequency,
family size, or neighboring-row content -- none of those signals are used
anywhere in the classification logic. Master-precedent percentages from
earlier phases were NOT used to decide state; they are cited only as
supporting confirmation where relevant.

This is NOT literally "an LLM read all 14,282 rows one at a time." It is a
dictionary-and-decomposition heuristic, iteratively corrected against the
actual data. That correction process is itself part of this audit's findings
(Quality Control section) and is disclosed in full rather than hidden.

Bugs found and fixed during this pass (in order):
 1. "arm," "leg," and "body" were missing from the anatomy dictionary
    entirely -- an early run classified literals like "arm right cloth 3a"
    and "Body Tone" as merely "referent unclear" (state B) because the most
    basic body-region words weren't recognized at all. Fixed by adding them;
    this alone moved several hundred families from B into A.
 2. Body-shape descriptor words (bulge, size, muscular, tone...) were
    initially treated as a full "referent" on their own, so a bare literal
    like "Length" or "Body Size" without any other context could reach state
    A. Descriptor words were reclassified as modifiers only, not referents by
    themselves; a bare descriptor with no anatomical/object anchor now
    correctly falls to state C, matching the spec's own "Front, Base, Root,
    Adjust, Main" example category.
 3. Directional-only literals (e.g. "UpDown") were falling to D (opaque)
    because "up"/"down" were treated as fully neutral rather than as real,
    if referent-less, English words. Fixed to resolve to C.
 4. A jargon/abbreviation word (bone, joint, ctrl, null, rig) plus a bare
    number was initially conflated with genuine descriptive words (front,
    base, root) into one "C" bucket. Per the task's own worked examples
    (Bone103, Bone.017, Bone001 should be D; Front/Base/Root/Adjust/Main
    should be C), these were split into two different states.
 5. "ValveBiped" systematically mis-tokenized: camelCase splitting breaks it
    into "Valve"+"Biped" before either half could match a glued
    "valvebiped" dictionary entry, so every "ValveBiped.*" literal (282
    residual families) was carrying two false "unknown" fragments and
    getting pulled down to C even when the actual body-part word (e.g. Vag,
    Ring, Thigh) was perfectly clear. Fixed by listing "valve" and "biped"
    separately.
 6. "wep" (weapon-prop marker) had been placed in the same "neutral prefix"
    bucket as genuine identity-only rig codes (bip, flm, mar...). Unlike
    those, "wep" carries real semantic content ("this is a weapon part") and
    was silently letting unrelated words underneath it (e.g. "strap" in
    "WEP_topstrap") default to a body/clothing destination. Removed from the
    neutral set so weapon-prefixed literals are judged on their own merits
    (typically state D or B, not a false Clothing/Anatomy state A).
 7. Missing vocabulary discovered by direct sampling and added: pigtail,
    lid/eyesocket/lash family, choker/flower/loincloth/chain/lace/fur
    (clothing), boobs/balls/cock/shaft/foreskin/nip/pussy/vag/clit/dick
    (genital synonyms actually used in this corpus), head/skull, and
    blink/squint/wink/smile/sneer/laugh/cry/etc. (facial-expression words
    previously present in the dictionary but never wired into the
    destination-illustration mapping).
Known remaining limitations (not fixed, disclosed instead of hidden):
 - Misspellings are not corrected (e.g. "theethU" for "teeth" was not
   recognized and the family was left at D).
 - The compound-word splitter only tries a two-way split, so a
   three-morpheme concatenation like "rbigtoe" (r+big+toe) is not resolved
   and the family lands in D despite containing a real word.
 - Specialized/Latin anatomical terms not in the general dictionary (e.g.
   "Flexor Radialis") are scored as D even though a domain expert would
   recognize them immediately.
 - Single-letter abbreviations (the "F" in "Bip_FMiddle01_R", almost
   certainly "Finger") are treated as neutral glue rather than resolved,
   which is conservative (doesn't force a possibly-wrong reading) but means
   a small amount of real information is left on the table.
These limitations bias the result toward UNDER-counting A and OVER-counting
D/C relative to what a careful human reader would do -- i.e. the true
readable fraction of the residual is very likely somewhat HIGHER than the
numbers below, not lower.

3-4. HOW MUCH OF THE RESIDUAL IS ACTUALLY READABLE / TAXONOMY-CLEAR?
==============================================================================
Semantically readable (A + B): 8,339 families / 8,410 literals = 58.4% of
the residual. The literal itself supplies enough information to understand
what the control is or does for well over half of what remains.

Immediately taxonomy-clear without further doctrine work (A only): 4,606
families / 4,636 literals = 32.3% of the residual -- meaning roughly one in
three remaining families could, in principle, go straight to a precedent-
and-contamination check (the normal Phase 1 method) rather than needing any
open research question first.

C (12.1%) and D (29.5%) together (41.6%) is where the "difficult long tail"
framing genuinely still applies -- but that is well under half of the
residual, not "most of it."

6. RECURRING DOCTRINE QUESTIONS THAT DOMINATE STATE B
==============================================================================
    2,879 families -- "core referent is clear but the literal carries an
           additional unclear fragment; needs closer reading before a final
           destination is picked" (e.g. taurenmale_hd_bone_136, SK_R_B_leg_4,
           Flm-ArmB-Float-B1). This is the largest B bucket and is mostly a
           "needs one more look, not a policy question" category -- many of
           these will resolve to A on individual inspection.
      405  families contain a technical-but-meaningful marker (twist,
           helper, corrective, deform, jiggle, attach) combined with a clear
           anatomical/object referent (e.g. r_shoulder_twist_c_helper,
           ValveBiped.Bip01_R_UpperarmTwist2, LeftDeltoid_helper,
           Body-L-Ass-Spread). Recurring doctrine question: where do
           corrective/helper bones belong when the anatomy they help is
           obvious -- with the anatomy group, or with a Correctives/
           RigHelpers destination? (The Master philosophy already has an
           answer in principle -- "object-specific purpose takes
           precedence" -- but each family should still be checked against
           it individually.)
      358  families are a recognizable object/prop noun with no body/
           clothing referent (Blade.R, Box08, Sheath 2-adjacent items).
           Recurring doctrine question: does Master want any kind of
           animator-facing Props destination, or does this material stay
           unresolved indefinitely? This is a real policy gap, not a
           classification gap.
       91  families contain a non-human creature/species referent
           (taurenBack and the Canine/Crow/taurenmale population).
           Recurring doctrine question: identical to the one already raised
           in PHASE2_ADVERSARIAL_PLAN_REVIEW.txt Part B -- does Master want
           a creature-anatomy destination at all? Confirmed again here from
           a completely different analysis method (word-reading rather than
           precedent-percentage), which strengthens the finding rather than
           merely repeating it.

7. WHAT KIND OF MISSING CONTEXT DOMINATES STATE C
==============================================================================
      821  families mix one recognizable generic/technical word with one
           unclear fragment (e.g. moustache right control, turret_root,
           Pincer Right Root, front_inwards) -- genuinely borderline; a
           closer read of a specific model or convention could move many of
           these to B or D, but not safely on the literal alone.
      537  families have a real referent word that is nonetheless
           outweighed by other unclear fragments (ForeskinTipPullBack,
           GenericHuman_l_loLidJnt, BellyInflateLeotard1) -- the referent
           is real but the literal's own construction makes the intended
           object ambiguous.
      370  families contain ONLY generic positional/functional/directional
           words with no anchor at all (RUp, right_16, _right_z_up, top-4)
           -- these are the cleanest illustration of the task's own C
           definition: real, readable English words, but structurally
           incapable of resolving to a specific universal destination
           without a referent.
The common thread: state C is not "hard to read" -- every sample above is
made of ordinary words. It is "readable, but the object being acted on is
missing from the literal itself," which is a fundamentally different
problem from state D.

specialized medical terms, weapon-prop vocabulary) rather than genuine
opacity -- the true opaque population is very likely smaller than 29.5%.

==============================================================================
9. WAS THE PRIOR "DIFFICULT LONG TAIL" DESCRIPTION MATERIALLY MISLEADING?
==============================================================================
Yes, materially. The prior census and adversarial review were built entirely
from ASCII-family singleton rate (99.0% singleton), prefix/stem recurrence,
naming-system concentration, and Master-precedent percentages. All of those
are properties of SPELLING, not of MEANING. This audit shows that reading
the actual words in the literal -- which was never done at scale in the
prior passes -- resolves 58.4% of the residual to a readable state
immediately, including 32.3% that already look genuinely taxonomy-clear.
None of the prior passes' mechanical findings were WRONG (the singleton
statistic, the low bip/root/helper concentration percentages, the "no
model provenance" finding are all still true and still matter for HOW to
proceed) -- but the prior passes' overall framing ("this is a difficult
long tail," "start with the naming systems that look established") answered
the wrong question. The right first question, as this task's premise states,
is "what does this literal mean," and that question was previously
under-asked.

==============================================================================
10. CONCLUSIONS TO WITHDRAW OR REVISE FROM EARLIER PHASE 2 OUTPUT
==============================================================================
WITHDRAW:
  - Any implication that the residual is predominantly hard-to-classify.
    58.4% reads clearly; that is a majority, not a "long tail exception."
  - The framing that bip*/ValveBiped*/Bip01* systems are useful primarily as
    a NAMING-SYSTEM signal. Fixing the ValveBiped tokenization bug (Section
    Methodology, item 5) shows the individual literals under that prefix are
    frequently perfectly clear once the prefix itself is read as neutral
    rather than as an unknown fragment blocking the rest of the reading.

REVISE (still true, but incomplete without this audit):
  - "99% singleton-family-ness implies a difficult long tail" -- true about
    spelling uniqueness, false as a difficulty signal; this audit is the
    concrete demonstration the adversarial review's question 9 predicted.
  - "Master precedent percentages are the primary evidence source" -- still
    correct for CONFIRMING a destination once meaning is established, but
    this audit shows word-level reading should come FIRST, with precedent
    checking as the second, confirmatory step -- not the first filter.
  - The adversarial review's ranked investigation order (clothing tokens,
    then adult-content body-morphs, then Bip01 anatomical set...) remains
    directionally reasonable, but should now be re-scoped around STATE-A
    FAMILIES specifically (4,606 of them, already domain-tagged in the
    companion TSV) rather than around broad tokens -- the token-based
    regions in the census/adversarial review and the family-level A/B states
    in this audit are two different cuts of the same data and should be
    cross-referenced, not treated as competing conclusions.

CONFIRMED, NOT WITHDRAWN:
  - No per-model provenance exists in the corpus (unaffected by this audit).
  - The creature/character taxonomy-scope question is real and unresolved
    (confirmed again here via the 91 creature-referent B families, by a
    completely independent method).
  - A genuine Props-destination policy gap exists (confirmed again here via
    the 358 "recognizable object/prop noun" B families).
  - A real opaque/technical population exists and is large (D = 29.5%,
    consistent in kind, if not in exact size, with earlier findings).

==============================================================================
QUALITY CONTROL: SPECIFIC FAILURE-MODE AUDIT
==============================================================================
- "Obvious literal demoted for being singleton": structurally impossible in
  this classifier -- family size is never read by classify_family(). No
  instance found or possible.
- "Clear literal demoted for heterogeneous prefix": structurally impossible
  -- no prefix-frequency or naming-system statistic is used anywhere in the
  per-family logic. Confirmed by the bip_l_wing/bip_Ear3_L/bip_pigtail_L_2
  worked examples from the task prompt, all of which now resolve to A once
  "bip" is read as a neutral rig marker rather than as an unknown blocker.
- "Technical-looking but explicit literal treated as opaque": found and
  fixed (helper_forearm_L vs bare helper01 test case; DEF-foot.L.002
  resolves to A while bare Bone017 resolves to D, matching the task's own
  worked examples exactly).
- "Vague literal promoted because of neighboring rows": structurally
  impossible -- no row has access to any other row's data.
- "Family assigned by substring while the full literal contradicts it":
  checked directly by sampling 40 of the 276 compound-splits this pass
  performed (e.g. upperlips->upper+lips, fingerbase->finger+base,
  necktie->neck+tie, bowtie->bow+tie, cockring->cock+ring); all 40 were
  correct decompositions with no contamination found. The compound-splitter
  requires BOTH halves of a two-way split to be independently real
  dictionary words, which is a strong protection against a spurious partial
  match.
- "Semantic interpretation inferred from neighboring rows": structurally
  impossible, same reason as above.
Two real defects WERE found and are disclosed rather than hidden: the
ValveBiped camelCase-tokenization bug and the "wep" false-neutrality bug
(both described under Methodology above, both fixed before the final run
that produced the numbers in this report).

VERDICT
==============================================================================
PRIOR PHASE 2 DIFFICULTY ASSESSMENT: OVERSTATED

Not "substantially correct" -- the prior census and adversarial review
never disputed a single mechanical fact this audit produced (family counts,
precedent percentages, and the no-provenance finding all still stand), but
their combined framing left the impression that most of the 14,282-family
residual was intrinsically hard to work with. Reading the actual literals
shows 58.4% resolves to an understandable meaning immediately, and roughly
one in three families (32.3%) already looks taxonomy-clear without any open
policy question attached.

Not "severely overstated" either -- 29.5% of the residual genuinely is
opaque by any reasonable reading (bare numbered technical placeholders,
uninterpretable codes, generic mesh/prop remnants), and the doctrine
questions raised in state B (creature-anatomy scope, technical-helper
placement, a possible Props destination) are real, unresolved, and will
still require deliberate human decisions before any bulk import -- the
difficulty was real, just not as large a share of the total as previously
implied.

No individual family was classified for import during this task.
sfm_defaultanimationgroups.txt was not edited.
