==============================================================================
PHASE 2 SEMANTIC PILOT -- 500 FAMILIES
Architecture under test: Python = corpus management/validation/bookkeeping;
Claude = actual per-family semantic judgment.
Companion data file: PHASE2_SEMANTIC_PILOT_500.tsv
==============================================================================

DESIGN AS EXECUTED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- 14,282 residual ASCII families were deterministically shuffled with a fixed,
  documented seed (Python random.Random(20260831)) and the first 500 were
  selected as the pilot population. All exact spellings of a family stayed
  together as one unit.
- The shuffle genuinely broke lexical adjacency: batch 1's first five families
  were eyeblink_right_out, five_bullet00, right index finger 1 grip 1,
  liplower_right2, wing_left_1_1 -- five unrelated domains in a row, with no
  visible run of similar bip_/Cape_/Bone_-style neighbors anywhere sampled.
- The 500 were split into five fixed batches of 100. For each batch, Claude
  was given ONLY the fold key and exact spelling(s) -- no OLD mechanical A/B/
  C/D label, no dictionary/rule-engine output, and no neighboring-batch
  context -- and produced a fresh semantic_status, semantic_referent,
  taxonomy_status, proposed_destination (only when taxonomy_status=CLEAR),
  confidence, uncertainty_reason, and a short literal-specific reason for
  every one of the 100 families in that batch.
- Python's role was confined to: building the shuffled selection, writing
  batch input files, and validating each returned batch -- checking row
  count, exact fold-key coverage (no missing, no invented keys), enum-field
  validity, and that every proposed destination is a real path in the
  current live Master's 34 valid group paths. Zero semantic decisions were
  made by Python at any point. All five batches validated cleanly on the
  first attempt: 100/100 keys returned, 0 missing, 0 hallucinated, 0
  malformed enum or destination values.

Headline categories:

- OLD D, NEW semantic CLEAR: 61 families (12.2% of the pilot). These are
  exactly the cases the dictionary-classifier's own limitations predicted:
  misspellings (penus 1 -> "penis", tongehide -> "tongue", bip fring00 l ->
  "fringe"), weapon-prop vocabulary the dictionary never covered (silencer,
  wep_wick2, wep_shell5, l_rear_holster_0u_8v), recognizable prop nouns
  (magnifier, torch3, condom 4, btn.hour), and creature/costume appendage
  words absent from the dictionary (feather 1a, feather 2a, pincer right
  mid, chocobocharm). Every one of these has a plainly readable meaning to
  a human (or a language model actually reading the word), which the static
  dictionary simply never learned.

- OLD B, NEW taxonomy CLEAR: 57 families (11.4%). The old classifier's B
  bucket conflated "genuinely needs a policy decision" with "the dictionary
  didn't have quite enough words to auto-map a destination." Reading the
  full literal resolved many of these immediately and confidently: eyes_
  symmetric -> Face/Eyes, sk_tail03 -> Tail, pussy_tighten -> Body Morphs,
  opt_as_l_upleg_b -> Legs, bobcut.rear_left_3_jiggle -> Hair. None of these
  actually raise a doctrine question; they were only "uncertain" because a
  static word list didn't have "eyes_symmetric" or "opt_as" pre-registered.

- OLD A, NEW semantic NOT fully clear: 12 families (2.4%). Small and mostly
  benign -- e.g. skull_06, upperhead, m_head_2, r_head_3, head back 1 were
  mechanically mapped straight to a destination by the old dictionary's
  simple word-priority list, but genuine reading recognizes that a bare
  "head" bone raises a real destination choice (Body? Face/Expressions?
  something else?) that the old system's shallow lookup could not see.
  bip01_*_thigh013/upperarm013/forearm007/hand007-style numbered sub-bones
  show the same pattern: the old dictionary happily mapped "thigh" straight
  to Legs, while genuine reading flags the primary-anatomy-vs-corrective-
  bone doctrine question the Master's own philosophy actually cares about.
  This is the OLD system being too confident, not the new one being wrong.

- OLD A, NEW taxonomy NOT clear: 38 families (7.6%, overlapping partly with
  the 12 above). Dominated by two recurring, principled patterns: (a) 23
  cases where the old dictionary mapped a numbered biped sub-bone (thigh,
  upperarm, forearm, hand, spine) straight to an anatomical group without
  recognizing the Correctives-doctrine question Master's own philosophy
  raises for such bones; (b) several bare "chain"/positional-only literals
  the old classifier's cruder morph-only-referent rule let through as A
  when they genuinely lack an owning object.

50 representative disagreements are shown in the two sample blocks above and
in PHASE2_SEMANTIC_PILOT_500.tsv (batch_id column lets you find any of the
500 by batch; cross-reference against PHASE2_SEMANTIC_READABILITY_AUDIT.tsv
by fold_key for the old state).

CONSISTENCY AUDIT (performed strictly AFTER independent judgment, per
instruction -- not used to revise any individual family's judgment)
==============================================================================
Python grouped the 500 pilot families by shared stem/pattern and flagged any
cluster with more than one destination or taxonomy_status. Flags found, with
an assessment of whether the variation looks principled or arbitrary:

- cape* (6 families): 100% consistent (Clothing, CLEAR). No flag.
- bare chain_N (5 families): 100% consistent (NEEDS_CONTEXT, no destination
  forced). No flag.
- *twist* bones (11 families): 100% consistent (NEEDS_DOCTRINE). No flag.
- bare numbered Bone### (12 families): 100% consistent (NOT_APPLICABLE).
  No flag.
- horn* (2 families): 100% consistent (NEEDS_DOCTRINE). No flag.
- *finger* (15 families): FLAGGED -- 13 resolved to a clear Fingers
  destination (with correct Left/Right/side-neutral routing based on each
  literal's own side marker, including finger2a correctly routed to the
  side-neutral groupFile/Fingers because it carries no L/R marker at all),
  while 2 ("...finger_globe..." entries) were held at NEEDS_CONTEXT because
  of an unusual "globe" qualifier not present in the other 13. This reads as
  a principled, literal-driven distinction, not noise -- but is reported as
  a flag per instruction, for human confirmation.
- creature (canine/crow/taurenmale, 8 families): FLAGGED -- 1 (canine_tail2)
  resolved CLEAR to Tail (Master already treats Tail as an established non-
  human-appendage destination), while the other 7 (specific limb/eye/brow/
  phalanges bones with no Tail-equivalent precedent, plus 2 bare taurenmale
  numbered bones with no named body part at all) stayed NEEDS_DOCTRINE or
  NOT_APPLICABLE. Explainable by genuine differences between the literals;
  flagged for human confirmation rather than silently accepted.
- *head*/*skull* (11 families): FLAGGED -- families naming a SPECIFIC facial
  sub-feature alongside "head" (head eyeball middle, bip faceforeheadtop l,
  head chin middle, head cheeck left 4) or using "head" in an unrelated
  sense (headgear_r.004 = clothing, cock-head-skinny = penis anatomy)
  resolved CLEAR with a specific destination; families using bare/generic
  "head" with no sub-feature (upperhead, head back 1, m_head_2, r_head_3,
  headfurtop.r) stayed NEEDS_DOCTRINE. Explainable, flagged for confirmation.
- unused-prefixed (13 families): FLAGGED -- the 4 that also name a real body
  part (dogmeat_eye_r, handr, ankle2helper, faceroot) went NEEDS_DOCTRINE;
  the 9 that are bare numbered/dummy/duplicate placeholders went
  NOT_APPLICABLE. Explainable by the presence/absence of an actual
  anatomical referent; flagged for confirmation.
- weapon-prop (wep_/v_weapon, 11 families): FLAGGED -- the 2 v_weapon.*_
  index/middle03 entries (finger-shaped names on a weapon view-model) went
  NEEDS_CONTEXT (is this a real posable finger or an internal viewmodel
  helper?); the other 9 plain weapon-component names went NEEDS_DOCTRINE
  (a cleaner "does Master want a Props destination" question). Explainable,
  flagged for confirmation.
- *jiggle* (9 families): FLAGGED -- the 4 where the jiggling OBJECT is
  explicitly named (a pendant, a hairstyle, a hat, a hair side-lock) resolved
  CLEAR to the object's own destination; the 5 with no named object (bare
  jiggle4/jiggle05/h3jiggle06, and two rig-code entries with only a vague
  "side"/"front" position) stayed NEEDS_CONTEXT. Explainable, flagged for
  confirmation.

No cluster showed destination disagreement that looked arbitrary or
attention-related (e.g. the same exact pattern getting different answers for
no visible reason). Every flagged cluster's variation traces to a real,
identifiable difference in the literal itself. None were auto-corrected, per
instruction -- they are reported here as review flags only.

==============================================================================
QUALITY ASSESSMENT
==============================================================================
- Equal attentiveness across batches 1-5: per-batch semantic_status counts
  were stable throughout -- CLEAR ranged 67-74 per 100, OPAQUE 13-24,
  PARTIAL 9-14; taxonomy CLEAR ranged 46-51 per 100 in every batch. No
  downward drift in CLEAR-rate or upward drift in OPAQUE-rate from batch 1
  to batch 5 was observed, which is what a batch size that preserves genuine
  per-item attention should look like.
- Short reasons reference the literal's own meaning: spot-checked across all
  five batches (e.g. "'Inflat' reads as truncated 'Inflate'", "'lid' is the
  recognizable eyelid referent", "no object-type word at all, purely
  positional"). Reasons are literal-specific, not templated boilerplate.
- Abstention is real, not decorative: 256 of 500 families (51.2%) received
  no proposed destination at all, spanning three different abstention
  reasons (NEEDS_DOCTRINE, NEEDS_CONTEXT, NOT_APPLICABLE) rather than one
  catch-all "unsure" bucket. Genuinely ambiguous single-word or acronym
  literals (lock1, plate 2, stick, c4, f7, ex_2, g8, glass_d3_d) were left
  OPAQUE/NOT_APPLICABLE rather than guessed at.
- Obvious readable literals recognized despite being unusual or misspelled:
  penus 1 -> penis, tongehide -> tongue, left_stomach_buldge -> bulge,
  head cheeck left 4 -> cheek, eye-r-nuetral -> neutral, lidubberopenl ->
  upper (all correctly read through the typo); Japanese-anatomical-term
  literals (hiji_r__rot_ude_r__z50__offset = right elbow, sakotsu_rt_rside_
  d__offset = right clavicle) were also correctly recognized rather than
  scored opaque for using non-English rig vocabulary.
- Generic prefixes were not used as a substitute for reading the rest of the
  name: bip_l_wing, Bone_eye_L, DEF-brows2_r, and DEF-thigh.r.004 all
  resolved CLEAR on their real referent (wing/eye/brows/thigh) despite
  carrying the same heterogeneous bip_/Bone_/DEF- prefixes that, taken
  alone, predict nothing. Conversely, when a "bip_"/"Bone_"/"unused"-
  prefixed literal genuinely had nothing else in it (bone077, bone.001_l,
  unusedbone091, bone._l), it was correctly scored OPAQUE rather than
  defaulted to some guessed anatomy -- confirming the prefix-neutral
  behavior works in both directions, not just the convenient one.
- Taxonomy assignments remained consistent across independently-judged
  batches: cape*, chain_N, *twist*, and bare Bone### clusters (spanning all
  five batches) were each internally 100% consistent despite being judged
  in entirely separate batch calls with no cross-batch memory.

==============================================================================
VERDICT
==============================================================================
SEMANTIC BATCH ARCHITECTURE: READY TO SCALE

No modification is required to the core mechanism before scaling to the
remaining 13,782 families. The pilot demonstrates:
  - genuine per-literal reading (not dictionary lookup) -- proven directly by
    the 61 old-D/new-CLEAR and 57 old-B/new-CLEAR disagreement categories,
    which are exactly the failure modes a static dictionary cannot fix and a
    reading model can;
  - stable attention across five independently-processed batches with no
    quality drift;
  - real, non-decorative abstention on genuinely ambiguous literals;
  - internally consistent taxonomy judgments on repeated patterns despite
    zero cross-batch context;
  - review flags (not silent auto-correction) surfaced on every cluster
    where a pattern legitimately split, exactly as instructed.

Two minor, low-cost process refinements are worth carrying into the full run
(neither is a correctness problem, both are efficiency/traceability notes):
  1. The pilot's proposed_destination values were mapped to complete
     canonical paths only when a specific sub-branch was unambiguous from
     the literal itself (e.g. LeftFingers vs. RightFingers by explicit side
     marker; the side-neutral groupFile/Fingers when no side marker exists).
     This convention should be written down explicitly as a standing
     instruction for the full run rather than left implicit.
  2. Bare positional/generic-word literals (front, back, side, top, up,
     down alone) and bare technical-jargon-plus-number literals (bone/
     joint/ctrl/null + a number) were treated as two different OPAQUE
     sub-flavors by the judge's own reasoning but recorded under the same
     semantic_status value. If a future pass wants to distinguish "real
     word, no referent" from "no real word at all" as separate machine-
     readable categories, that would need a schema addition -- not required
     for the pilot's purpose, but worth deciding now rather than after
     14,282 rows are committed to the current three-value scheme.

No files were edited. No family beyond the 500-family pilot was classified.
