PROPOSED AMENDMENT V2-1 -- RULE 7 (Body Morphs), sharpen the structural-
vs-deformation test with a positive word list
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROBLEM FOUND: V1's Amendment 1 correctly added a root/plain-anatomy
carve-out to rule 7, but its own application overcorrected "nipple up" --
treating a bare directional word ("up") as equivalent to a plain
structural segment. The distinction needs a clearer positive test for
what counts as deformation/state language versus plain structural naming.

PROPOSED ADDITION to rule 7 (refines V1's "FURTHER CORRECTION" paragraph):

  "PLAIN STRUCTURAL ANATOMY (routes to Body, or the relevant anatomical
  group) includes: a bare body-part name, a numbered segment, a side
  marker alone, and an explicit 'root'/'parent'/'origin' bone
  designation -- none of these describe the visible form changing.
  Examples: RightNipple1, NippleChain.Left, penis root, Vag_L_2,
  Cock.Shaft_4.

  VISIBLE ACTION/DIRECTION/STATE/SHAPE APPLIED TO ANATOMY (routes to Body
  Morphs) includes a word describing the anatomy's form or position
  actively changing: up, down, forward, backward, movement, poke, pull,
  push, tighten, grip, open, close, inflate, deflate, bulge, wide,
  skinny, thick/thickness, shrink, muscular, tone, hard/erect, smack/
  impact-deformation, augmentation/'job', detail. Examples: NippleUp,
  NippleDown, Nipple Movement, NipplePoke, BreastUp, BellyIn, ButtPull,
  PenisForward.
  A bare directional word (up/down/forward/backward) attached to an
  anatomical noun IS deformation/state language, not a structural
  segment marker -- do not treat 'up' the same as a numbered segment or
  a side marker."

PROPOSED AMENDMENT V2-2 -- NEW RULE, abbreviation grounding standard
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROBLEM FOUND: the first audit upgraded several OPAQUE rows to PARTIAL by
expanding a short abbreviation to a plausible real word (Ex -> "helper"-
style EX suffix without an anatomical anchor, whl -> "wheel", acs ->
"accessory", hd -> "head") without requiring the expansion to be grounded
in anything beyond plausibility.

PROPOSED NEW RULE (to be numbered upon approval and insertion):

  "AN ABBREVIATION EXPANSION IS NOT SEMANTIC EVIDENCE UNLESS GROUNDED.
  Do not upgrade a literal's semantic_status merely because an
  abbreviation COULD plausibly expand to a meaningful word. An
  abbreviation may support a semantic interpretation only when its
  expansion is grounded by at least one of:
    - other explicit content in the SAME literal (e.g. RForeArmEX is
      readable because 'ForeArm' supplies the referent -- 'EX' alone
      inherits nothing from this by itself)
    - a recognized rig/DCC naming convention with SPECIFIC, not merely
      plausible, support
    - sibling spellings in the SAME corpus establishing the expansion
      (e.g. Key.S/Key.X/Key 17/Key.9 are grounded because Key.Black_3 and
      Key.White_25 already exist in the same pilot using the identical
      'Key.<label>' pattern)
    - strong, specific corpus evidence (a documented reference, not an
      inferred guess)
  A merely plausible expansion, with no such grounding, is not enough --
  leave the literal OPAQUE/NOT_APPLICABLE."

PROPOSED AMENDMENT V2-3 -- NEW RULE, model/character prefixes are not
anatomical words
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROBLEM FOUND: "TaurenMale_HD" was reinterpreted as containing the
anatomical word "head" (HD -> head) purely because that expansion was
possible, ignoring that TaurenMale_HD reads far more naturally as a
model/variant prefix in front of a long, contiguous, generically-numbered
bone series.

PROPOSED NEW RULE (to be numbered upon approval and insertion):

  "MODEL/CHARACTER PREFIX ABBREVIATIONS MUST NOT BE REINTERPRETED AS
  ANATOMICAL WORDS WITHOUT POSITIVE EVIDENCE. A prefix that names or
  abbreviates a model, character, or variant (e.g. TaurenMale_HD,
  Bip_STONE, fbx_sakura) is not itself anatomical content, and a short
  code within such a prefix must not be expanded into an anatomical word
  (e.g. 'HD' -> 'head') merely because that expansion is possible. This
  applies even when the surrounding literal is a long, contiguous,
  generically-numbered bone series (a strong signal that the prefix is a
  model/variant tag, not a body-region label). Unless the COMPLETE
  literal independently supplies anatomical content beyond the model/
  character prefix, leave the family OPAQUE/NOT_APPLICABLE. This rule is
  a specific application of the abbreviation-grounding standard (see
  Amendment V2-2) to model/character-prefix naming specifically."

PROPOSED AMENDMENT V2-4 -- NEW RULE, taxonomy does not require every
token to be decoded
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROBLEM FOUND: the pilot repeatedly withheld a destination for a family
whose PRIMARY owner was already clear (an explicit facial feature, limb,
or garment) merely because a SECONDARY qualifier, variant code, or
technical role could not be fully decoded -- conflating "not every token
is translated" with "the destination is unknown."

PROPOSED NEW RULE (to be numbered upon approval and insertion; this is a
standing principle, not limited to the examples below):

  "TAXONOMY DOES NOT REQUIRE EVERY TOKEN TO BE DECODED. If the PRIMARY
  semantic owner/referent is clear, an unresolved secondary qualifier
  does NOT block a taxonomy destination unless that qualifier creates a
  CREDIBLE COMPETING owner or category. A taxonomy destination asks 'what
  semantic thing does this control belong to' -- it does NOT require
  'can every token in the control name be translated.'
  Examples where the primary owner is reliable and the destination should
  NOT be withheld: 'C Dlipout' (lip is a reliable owner -> Face/Lower
  Face; failing to decode 'D'/'out' does not create a competing owner);
  'L Dlip B' (same); 'leg left StC' (leg left is reliable anatomy ->
  Legs; an unresolved variant/technical suffix does not itself block
  Legs); a bare finger-plus-unresolved-qualifier bone (the finger is the
  reliable owner even if a 'globe'/joint-type qualifier is unclear); a
  named garment plus an unresolved secondary descriptor, PROVIDED every
  plausible reading of that descriptor stays within the same destination
  category (e.g. 'belt hat' -- whether it means the belt or the hat, both
  are Clothing).
  Examples where the ambiguity DOES create a competing category, and
  destination should remain withheld: a bare word with no anchor at all
  ('chain', 'show', 'squish') that could belong to entirely different
  destinations depending on its unstated object; a real object whose
  plausible types span different destinations (e.g. a 'plate' that could
  be worn armor -> Clothing or a dinnerware prop -> Other; a 'lock' that
  could be Hair or Other; a 'stick' that could be a worn hair-stick
  accessory -> Clothing/Hair or a handheld prop -> Other).
  Also remember: uncertainty about whether a control is a DESIRABLE
  animator control is NOT the same as uncertainty about its semantic
  TAXONOMY. Helper/reference/internal-looking naming alone (e.g.
  '_reference', a weapon-viewmodel helper bone that also happens to be a
  normal named finger) is not evidence for Useless/IGNORE, and is not
  grounds to withhold a taxonomy destination when the owner is otherwise
  clear."

PROPOSED AMENDMENT V2-5 -- RULE (ownerless accessories), apply
consistently -- confirms V1's existing rule, no wording change needed
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
No new rule text is proposed here. The V1 rulebook's existing guidance
(bare accessory terms with no named owner should not default to Clothing
merely because the item CAN be worn) was already correct; the defect
found in V2 was an APPLICATION inconsistency (ChocoboCharm and the "bow"
rows were not held to the same standard as Strap/Tassel/Band in the same
audit), not a gap in the rule itself. Future batches should mechanically
re-check every Clothing-destination row for a bare, ownerless decorative-
accessory noun (charm, bow, pendant, clasp, etc.) before finalizing,
rather than relying on a single pass to catch every instance.

PROPOSED AMENDMENT V2-6 -- RULE 16 (Other), REVISED holster wording
(supersedes V1's Amendment 3 proposal)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
STATUS: this policy question has now been SETTLED by explicit decision
(see PHASE2_SEMANTIC_PILOT_500_AUDIT_V2_REPORT.txt Section 6) and applied
to all 4 affected data rows. The rule text below is proposed to record
that settled decision in the standing rulebook; it revises V1's
Amendment 3, which proposed the same DIRECTION (Clothing default) but had
not yet been adopted as policy.

PROPOSED REPLACEMENT for the holster portion of rule 16:

  "HOLSTERS: a holster is treated as WORN EQUIPMENT and routes to
  groupFile/Clothing by default when the literal describes the holster
  as character equipment/body-positioned gear (e.g. Gear.Holster,
  l_side_holster_*, l_rear_holster_*, Holster_Base) -- Clothing, NOT
  Other, unless the COMPLETE literal positively establishes that the
  holster is a component of a specific weapon/prop ASSEMBLY rather than
  worn character equipment (e.g. explicitly named as a sub-part of a
  particular gun/sword prop hierarchy). This is the settled default;
  apply it going forward without re-litigating it per family."

PROPOSED AMENDMENT V2-7 -- RULE 7 (Body Morphs), lube/wetness scope
clarification
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
STATUS: SETTLED for the single case tested (bip_lube.001 -> Other) and
proposed here as a narrow, non-generalizing clarification.

PROPOSED ADDITION to rule 7:

  "LUBE/WETNESS/FLUID EFFECTS ARE NOT BODY MORPHS MERELY BECAUSE THEY ARE
  ADULT-CONTENT OR ANATOMY-ADJACENT. A bare lube/wetness/fluid-effect
  control (e.g. bip_lube.001) is an effect/material/fluid referent, not
  structural anatomy and not anatomical shape/deformation morphology --
  it routes to groupFile/Other, not Body Morphs, on that basis. Do NOT
  generalize this into 'every control containing wet is Other' -- if the
  COMPLETE literal clearly describes an anatomical geometry/state change
  (e.g. an explicit swelling, opening, or shape word applied to a named
  body part) alongside a wetness word, classify according to that full
  anatomical meaning instead."

PROPOSED AMENDMENT V2-8 -- RULE 18/19, do not infer Wings from species
alone
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROBLEM FOUND: V1's Amendment 4 correctly flagged phalanges as wing-vs-
foot ambiguous for a bird, but the same audit pass left Crow_3.Clavical_R
at Wings on the reasoning "a crow's only forelimbs are its wings, so any
crow shoulder-girdle bone must be wing-associated" -- this is exactly the
"infer Wings because the animal is a bird" reasoning that should have
been rejected consistently. It has now been corrected (Crow_3.Clavical_R
downgraded to NEEDS_CONTEXT).

PROPOSED ADDITION to rule 18/19 (extends V1's Amendment 4 wording):

  "DO NOT INFER WING OWNERSHIP MERELY BECAUSE THE ANIMAL IS A BIRD (OR
  ANY OTHER FLIGHTED CREATURE). A bone routes to Wings only when the
  COMPLETE LITERAL, or an established and documented skeletal-taxonomy
  reference for that specific model, sufficiently identifies it as part
  of the flight appendage -- not merely because the species in question
  has no other forelimb type. A clavicle, humerus, ulna, or similar
  shoulder-girdle/forelimb bone on a bird is NOT automatically Wings by
  elimination; if the literal does not itself say 'wing'/'flight'/name a
  wing-specific segment, and no other established evidence resolves it,
  use NEEDS_CONTEXT rather than Wings. Femur is an exception: a femur has
  no wing-vs-other competing reading in any vertebrate (it is always a
  hindlimb/leg bone), so femur routes to Legs without this caution
  applying."
